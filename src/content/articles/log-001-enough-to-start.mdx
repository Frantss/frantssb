I finally decided on creating a web portfolio. Something to share my journey as an engineer, a record of sorts, a place for logging myself.

Then, as soon as I started, I realized that there's not much to show off: a bunch of unfinished projects, plenty of scrambled thoughts and opinions that were never put down in writing, countless pieces of knowledge learned that were never shared.

So I'm 7+ years (eleven if we count college) into software development, with no actual log or record of what I've accomplished, my learnings, my mistakes, who I am as an engineer.

The first thought that crossed my mind is something you might already be quite familiar with: impostor syndrome, the feeling of not having accomplished anything of true value, not having enough to show for it, or not being at the skill level you're supposed to be at.

But instead of trusting this feeling, I tried to focus on gathering what I did have: [safeish](https://github.com/Frantss/safeish), a result-based error-handling solution; [oxform](https://github.com/Frantss/oxform), a type-safe and framework-agnostic form library; a couple of local client websites in the [marketing](https://altereco.com.uy) and [food](https://atenea-coffee.com) industries; and some [old projects](https://frantss.uy/projects#miscellaneous), stuff I built back when I was a junior first starting in the industry. Not a lot, but enough. Enough to start, to start logging myself, my journey.

Starting today, I'll be using this space to share my opinions, things I learn on the job, and whatever I build.

So this is my first post. The first entry in the log. And if you are reading this from a similar place, remember to show your work, put yourself out there, and log yourself. You're probably a better engineer than you think, and you can be even greater.
